Nika lets you put AI workflows in an application, describe their permitted
effects, and inspect what happened after a run. This page separates what you
can use in 0.123 from the earlier plan the project keeps as a record.

## What does “Compile” mean here?

Offline, with no model, `nika compile` writes exact skeletons
(`nika compile --list` names them), bounded support intents and conservative
edits of an existing file (`--base` with `--change`). An intent it cannot
settle stays incomplete and asks its questions; it never writes a substitute.

For a request in your own words, open the [terminal Session](/getting-started/session)
(`nika`) or run `nika compile "<request>" --authoring-model <provider/model>`.
A model you choose writes the candidate, and Nika checks it, and rehearses it
where it can, before anything is saved or run. Authoring with a large model can
take minutes, and a rehearsal skips model steps:
[Known limits in 0.123](/changelog/known-limits) lists what to expect.

Start with [the offline authoring path](/examples/bounded-authoring). The
[engine roadmap](https://github.com/supernovae-st/nika/blob/main/ROADMAP.md) and
[public engine issues](https://github.com/supernovae-st/nika/issues) track the
maintenance work.

## What happens after 0.123?

0.123 is the open-source reference release. From here the project is
maintained: security fixes, bug fixes and compatibility. No major new
development is planned, and no 1.0 release is planned. The engine roadmap and
the specification keep the conditions once set for a 1.0 release as the record
of that plan, not as a commitment:

- **Release and user experience qualification:** installation, documentation,
  editor setup and supported execution paths must work together.
- **The contract system:** everything that crosses a boundary carries a
  declared contract (types, errors, effects and bounds), and unknown
  information stays visible instead of being reported as success.
- **Long-term compatibility:** conformance, archives, deterministic checking,
  a compatibility promise and succession are launch conditions.
- **Design-partner release candidates:** the plan placed these before a
  launch.

Memory, retrieval and cognition were never a single launch condition: the
former Connectome umbrella, which required the whole memory subsystem before
1.0, was retired on 2026-10-08.

These were conditions, not delivery dates or claims of completion. The
[owning engine roadmap](https://github.com/supernovae-st/nika/blob/main/ROADMAP.md)
and the specification's
[timeline](https://github.com/supernovae-st/nika-spec/blob/main/timeline/timeline.yaml)
carry the detailed decisions. [Timeline and evidence](/reference/timeline)
explains how to read them without confusing a proposal with a shipped feature.
